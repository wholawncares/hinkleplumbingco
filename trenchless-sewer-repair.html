What Trenchless Sewer Repair Covers in Belleville, IL

Trenchless repair addresses a damaged lateral with lining or bursting through access points, instead of opening the full length of the run. Each item below describes part of the work, from inspection and planning through restoration of access points and a check of the finished line.

01

Camera Inspection and Location

A camera run shows cracks, root entry, offsets, and sags, and locates the line and its depth. Findings separate a spot problem from a line-wide condition and guide whether trenchless work, open-trench repair, or another approach fits the line.

02

Pipe Lining Assessment

Lining places a pipe inside the existing line, where the host pipe holds its shape and alignment. The camera record, pipe material, offsets, and sags shape the assessment, and a collapsed section points away from lining toward another method.

03

Pipe Bursting Assessment

Bursting breaks the old pipe outward while pulling a replacement pipe into the same path. Soil, depth, nearby utilities, and access at each end shape whether bursting fits, and the camera record must show a path bursting can follow.

04

Access Points and Surface Care

Trenchless work uses access points at the ends of the run or at a cleanout, rather than opening the length. Driveways, walks, planting, and mature trees stay in place, although access areas need preparation, protection, and restoration after the work.

05

Property Line and City Program Check

Ownership of the lateral runs from the building to the city main, including beyond the property line. The City Sewer Lateral Repair Program may cover part of the outer portion, so check current City program details before assuming coverage.

06

Final Camera Check and Cleanup

After lining or bursting, a camera run checks the finished line for alignment and debris left in the pipe. Access points are backfilled and restored, and the findings and method used are reviewed with the homeowner before the work closes.

Methods Compared

Lining, Bursting, and Open-Trench Repair

Trenchless methods suit some lines and not others, and the camera record guides the choice. The City of Belleville requires a permit before plumbing work begins (§152.05(A)); direct replacement of a toilet, sink, faucet, or other appliance is exempt, except hot water heaters require an electrical or gas permit (§152.05(B)(3)).

01

Cured-in-Place Lining

A liner is inserted into the existing pipe and cured in place, forming a pipe inside the line. Lining suits lines that hold alignment and shape, with cracks or root entry, and it leaves joints sealed along the lined section.

02

Pipe Bursting

A bursting head breaks the pipe outward as a replacement pipe follows into the same path. Bursting differs from full replacement by open trench, although access points are opened at each end and the camera record must show a path.

03

Spot Lining for a Single Defect

A short liner covers a single crack, joint, or root-entry point, leaving the rest of the line untouched. Spot lining suits a line sound apart from one defect, and the camera record must show the defect clearly first.

04

Open-Trench Repair

Open-trench work excavates the damaged section, replaces the pipe, and backfills the trench. It suits collapsed pipe, severe offsets, or bellies that trenchless methods cannot follow, and it allows a look at soil, depth, and nearby lines during the work.

05

When Trenchless Does Not Fit

Collapsed pipe, severe offsets, bellies, and lines without a clear path rule out lining or bursting. In those cases, open-trench repair or replacement is discussed, with the camera findings explaining why a trenchless method was set aside for that line.

06

Backwater Valve Consideration

A backwater valve may be installed on a building drain where backflow could occur. Whether a valve fits depends on line layout, access, and camera findings, and any valve discussion stays separate from the trenchless method choice.
